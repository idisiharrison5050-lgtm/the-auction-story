# STORY BIBLE — THE AUCTION

## Core creative rule
The audience should become curious because of what they are watching, not because the story is explained to them. The cold open should create questions before answers.

The creator does not want the story spoiled in advance. Future planning should therefore describe production requirements and immediate actions without unnecessarily revealing later twists.

## Format
The project is being produced as a series of chapters. We are not trying to generate a complete 10-minute movie in one pass.

Current target: approximately 4 minutes for the first chapter, with longer chapters possible later as the workflow improves.

## Tone
- Cinematic and grounded.
- Suspenseful without relying on supernatural spectacle.
- Interesting first; genre can evolve naturally.
- Characters should feel like real people rather than stereotypes.
- Avoid obvious movie-villain behavior.
- Expensive, controlled visual language rather than chaotic AI spectacle.

## Cold open
The film opens inside a prestigious private auction.

The auction begins with ordinary valuable objects such as jewelry, paintings and antiques. Then the auctioneer presents **Lot 47**.

Lot 47 is a small, completely ordinary-looking wooden box.

### Established bidding sequence
These numbers are locked from the original story outline:

1. Bidding starts at **$500**.
2. The price reaches **$1,000**.
3. The price reaches **$5,000**.
4. Something strange happens: a man across the room bids **$100,000**.
5. The protagonist has no idea why the man wants the ordinary-looking box.
6. The protagonist bids again.
7. The stranger follows.
8. The price eventually reaches **$500,000**.
9. The protagonist has nowhere near that kind of money.
10. Then his phone rings.
11. The incoming number belongs to his **dead father**.
12. He answers.
13. The father's voice speaks.

Do not replace these established amounts with $150,000/$200,000/etc. Those were later improvisations and are not canon.

## Narrative structure
The opening should not immediately explain how the protagonist arrived at the auction. The first minute or two should function as a hook. The backstory can be revealed afterward.

## Production philosophy
We build the film shot-by-shot:

Reference/design → clean frame → image-to-video → review → keep or regenerate → next shot.

Do not ask an image model to invent an entire chapter when individual controlled shots will produce better continuity.

## Reference-image rule
If an existing character/reference image already contains the exact visual needed for an image-to-video shot, point the creator to that existing image/panel instead of unnecessarily generating another text-to-image image.

Example: `Use the Daniel close-up from the character board as the reference.`

If a new image is genuinely needed, provide a text-to-image prompt. The prompt itself should make the intended image clear to the creator.

If a reference board has labels/text, use the clean individual crop for the actual video model whenever practical. Labels are for human organization, not part of the film.
