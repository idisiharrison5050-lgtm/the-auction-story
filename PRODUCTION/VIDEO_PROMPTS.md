# VIDEO PROMPT RULES

## Image-to-video
Prompts should describe:
- camera movement
- character action
- natural environmental movement
- continuity requirements
- performance restraint

Avoid asking the video model to invent story beats that have already been decided.

## Audio
Unless the chosen video model specifically requires audio prompting, treat generated audio as provisional. Final dialogue, ambience and effects can be handled separately during editing.

## Existing references
When an existing image already contains the correct character, prop or composition, use that image as the reference rather than generating a new text-to-image frame.

The production instruction can simply identify the panel, for example:
`Use the Daniel close-up from the character board as the reference.`

If the existing image has organizational text, crop it away when practical before sending it to the video model.

## New references
When no existing image is suitable, provide a clear text-to-image prompt. The prompt should establish the exact composition, character, wardrobe, lighting, location and cinematic intent needed for the following video shot.

## Character safety/identity
All characters are original fictional designs. Do not intentionally model their faces after famous or real people. If a generated face appears too familiar or resembles another established character, regenerate it.

## Visual target
Realistic, premium cinematic thriller look. Controlled camera movement, believable human motion, natural lighting, no unnecessary spectacle.
